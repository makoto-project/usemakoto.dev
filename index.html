Know where data began, what changed, and who attested to it.

Makoto is a source-first, SLSA-like framework for data. Each origin and transformation is an immutable signed statement. A receiver verifies the exact graph, the authorized signers, private organizational rules, and the final data bytes.

Three questions, one portable handoff

The hard part is not identifying one source file. It is keeping its history attached after that file becomes ten copies across systems, teams, vendors, and forgotten exports. See why lineage matters.

Source

Where did this data begin?

An origin statement binds source claims to the exact collected bytes.

History

What happened along the way?

Every transformation points to exact predecessor statement and artifact digests, forming an append-only DAG.

Trust

Who attested to each claim?

DSSE signatures prove key control; the receiver's policy separately decides whether those keys are authorized.

The v0.2 flow

1. OriginHash source bytes and sign the source claim.
2. TransformBind each input to its predecessor statement and subject.
3. HandoffSign the exact statements, roots, heads, profiles, and final artifacts.
4. VerifyResolve pinned schemas, authorize signers, reconstruct the graph, and hash final bytes.

A small core; private rules remain private

Makoto defines origin, transformation, handoff, trust, and reporting primitives. Teams extend them with digest-pinned JSON Schema profiles that may constrain statement metadata, transformation fields, or structured output data. A schema can stay entirely inside an organization; a receiver resolves it offline by URI and exact digest.

Infinitely extensible

URI-namespaced extensions and private profiles add organizational meaning without forking the core wire format.

Append-only evidence

New signed statements reference earlier payload and artifact digests. Existing history is never edited in place.

Exact-byte integrity

Hashes bind the precise bytes presented. Reformatting, newline conversion, or a one-byte mutation intentionally changes the digest.

What verification does—and does not—prove

Can establishDoes not establish by itself
Signature validity and receiver-defined signer authorizationThat the signer was truthful or the claimed code actually ran
Hash-linked graph continuity and signed handoff completenessThat no newer valid handoff exists without an independent freshness anchor
Exact-byte integrity and JSON Schema conformanceConfidentiality, legal compliance, data quality, or absence of malicious content
